Actual option-chain test · 2017-01-03 to 2024-09-30

Harvest calm markets. Stay uncapped through recovery.

We redefined CAPE 20–30 as normal and above 30 as extreme, then tested a causal NAV recovery lockout. Five-delta calls collected a small positive premium edge in this sample. The most defensible implementation is selective, partially covered, and explicitly paused after a major NAV drawdown.

I · Is it realistic now?

Not with the present $10,000 account.

One standard covered call requires 100 shares. At the latest local closes, one fully covered SPY contract needs approximately $76,347 of SPY and one QQQ contract needs $70,632 of QQQ. A $10,000 account holds only about 13.1 SPY shares or 14.2 QQQ shares.

Do not solve the sizing problem with naked calls. XSP, call spreads, or broker-specific fractional option products are different strategies with basis, liquidity, settlement, and tail-risk differences. They should not be described as ordinary covered calls.

Above 30 is extreme—but OR still makes the gate broad.

What the test says: CAPE >30 OR calm volatility was active in 90.4% of SPY weeks and 89.6% of QQQ weeks, so it remains close to continuous overwriting. Calm volatility alone added 0.30% of annualized XIRR for SPY and 0.67% for QQQ in-sample. Adding the recovery lockout reduced active weeks to 54.9% and 46.8%; it sacrificed some premium, but specifically removes the covered-call cap during deep-drawdown recoveries.

The lockout is risk control, not a return optimizer. It did not maximize terminal wealth in this one sample. Its purpose is to avoid selling rebound convexity exactly when the deleveraged strategy needs it most.

VIII · Operating rule if tested live

Five delta, calm volatility, partial coverage—and a recovery lockout.

ValuationTreat CAPE 20–30 as normal and above 30 as extreme. CAPE is a slow context variable, not the weekly timing trigger.
RegimeUse prior-known 60-session VIX/VXN percentile below 70% as the primary call-sale gate.
RecoveryAfter NAV falls 10%, sell no calls until NAV regains its prior high and another 20 trading sessions pass.
EligibilitySell only against fully paid, unleveraged 100-share lots; cover initially only 25–50% of eligible shares.
PremiumCredit net—not gross—premium to Treasury. Reinvest it under the existing drawdown ladder instead of immediately buying more exposure at highs.
Stop conditionFreeze the rule for 52 trades; compare total portfolio return and net option P&L, not win rate.

My conclusion: use covered calls as a small cash-harvesting sleeve, not as the accelerator. The accelerator remains regular contributions, keeping rebound exposure uncapped, disciplined Treasury deployment in drawdowns, and limited valuation/NAV-governed leverage. Ten- and twenty-delta weekly overwriting remain rejected by this sample.
