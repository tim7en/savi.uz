I · Mechanical policy

Signals today, trades at the next close.

Quality entryAt the portfolio −40% rung, only names with 20 already-reported quarters and an intact earnings guardrail qualify.
5% at +20%Sell 5% of current shares whenever the prior close crosses the next multiplicative rung; send proceeds to reserve.
Five-year reviewCompute trailing five-year adjusted-total-return CAGR from data available at the signal close.
Two breaksClose only when CAGR is below 5% and TTM earnings are non-positive or persistently declining.

Threshold convention: “from the last harvest level” is literal. The +20% ladder is 1.20×, 1.44×, 1.728× and so on—not additive +20%, +40%, +60% from the original entry. The implementation uses adjusted close, so the ladder measures total return rather than price appreciation alone.

IV · The forever-hold question

The guardrail was too permissive to guarantee cleanup.

The baseline bought 51 lots across three crisis entries. By the final date, all 51 remained open; the oldest had been held 7.7 years. The full-period rule generated 308 partial harvests but no compounder exits.

Decision: the “CAGR below 5% AND earnings broken” rule is a quality-break exit, not a true sunset. If the operational goal is to ensure capital eventually rotates back to SPY, add an unconditional outer limit—such as exit at year 10—or change AND to OR. Both are materially different policies and should be tested before adoption.

VI · Bias audit

Trade timing is clean; stock selection is not.

Price thresholds use the prior close and execute at the next close. Five-year CAGR stops at the signal close. Earnings use report availability, not fiscal-period end: before-open reports may be used that day, while after-close or unknown-time reports are delayed to the next calendar day. Missing fundamentals never force an exit.

Unresolved bias: the 18 candidates are a present-day, survivor-selected basket; historical market-cap membership, delisted-company returns, and vintage fundamental snapshots are unavailable. The EPS vendor may also revise old observations. This is a temporally clean exit-policy comparison on fixed candidates—not a survivor-free estimate of stock-selection alpha.
