Six Proposals, One Survivor

Six ways to improve the book were tested in one sitting: a machine-learned trade filter, a leveraged 3:1 scalp, support and resistance zones, 20x leverage, a volatility-surprise trade, and option activity as a trend signal. Five died on their controls. The two things that turned out to matter were never about picking the trade at all.

Nothing picks the trade

A gradient-boosted model was fitted walk-forward on eighteen conditions known at entry — trend, momentum, volatility regime, channel extension, relative strength, market state — trained only on trades that had already closed, then used to decline trades before the six-slot cap saw them.

What the reversal said

At four hours the trades the model ranked worst outperformed the ones it ranked best. At sixty minutes the opposite. On the daily book the model beat its reversal and still lost to taking every trade. Three runs, three answers.

Capacity, not selection

The daily book offered 8,214 signals and the cap took 1,554. Filtering is nearly inert — the top-half filter still took 1,450 of the same slots. Rescored as a slot priority it reached 0.62 against 0.50, but the 4-hour replication came back at 0.77 against an incumbent of 0.81.

One condition survived everything: rs_rank, the name's trailing return against its peers. +0.064, +0.060 and +0.043 across the three books, and +0.087 in the bracket study — the same sign in all five sub-periods every time. It is also below 0.10, which is this programme's own bar for calling a feature proven.

The order type was worth more than everything else combined

A fixed 3:1 bracket held one to three sessions, swept across four stop distances and three holding windows on 42,808 fresh breakouts. Every cell lost money at taker cost, and the best cell was the widest stop with the longest window — the gradient runs back toward the book it was meant to replace.

A breakout entry is a buy-stop, which is marketable by construction. You cannot rest a limit above the market to buy strength, so the 5bp row is not a cheaper version of this strategy — it is a different strategy. The gap between 0.11 and 1.19 is not the bracket, the conditions, the interval or the leverage. It is the order type on one leg.

Why tight stops are the expensive ones

Cost in R is the round trip divided by the stop distance, and that ratio is leverage-invariant. At a 0.5N stop the distance is 0.230% of price, so a 10bp round trip costs 0.43R every trade. At 2N it is 0.911% and the same round trip costs 0.11R. Scalping does not make you nimble; it makes you pay a third of your risk unit in fees.

The option features behaved exactly as the earlier feature-power study predicted, and it did not help. High implied vol against its own norm cut the bracket's time-out rate from 22.8% to 13.3%; gamma balance and net GEX did the same. Mean R barely moved. A magnitude forecast makes the target and the stop more reachable in equal measure, so it converts unresolved trades into resolved ones that split in the same ratio. Magnitude without direction cannot create expectancy in a symmetric bracket.

Support and resistance did nothing, under both constructions

After a session closed two implied moves down, a limit rested below the market and targeted the nearest zone above it — the point of control or value-area high of a trailing volume profile. Confirmed pivot highs ran as an independent second definition of "zone", and a plain multiple of risk at the same median distance ran as the control.

The zone is decoration

Replacing it with a plain multiple of risk at the same distance did better, in both runs. And the two constructions disagree wildly — 1.11/1.68 for volume levels against 0.68/1.07 for pivots, with a completely different trade shape. A level that changes its answer when you change how you draw it is a level in the code, not in the market.

The stretch trigger is real

1.11 against a quiet-day null of 0.22, and 1.68 against 0.50. Resting the same limit on a random day earns a fraction of it. And realized vol did the job at least as well as the chain, which means the setup needs no option data and can be taken to the wider universe.

The literal "10% below" entry produced the most exciting number of the day and it was noise: +3.68 under implied vol on 116 trades, −0.12 under realized vol on 203. Same rule, different normaliser, opposite verdict. Every extreme Sharpe in these tables sits on a small-n cell.

Leverage is an output, and the denominator is the stop

A strategy scoring 1.68 still destroyed the account at 20x. That is not a contradiction. Position risk is size times stop distance, so fixing the size floats the risk — and across these names the stop spans a factor of 25, from 0.95% of price at the 5th percentile to 23.83% at the worst.

The trades that did it

RIVN on 2 March 2022: stop 15.24% of price, so at 3.33x per slot the position risked 50.8% of the account and lost 51.1% of it. Then COIN (−49.4%), MSTR (−46.4%), MSTR again (−35.0%), TSLA (−31.8%). Trough 3 January 2023.

Every one of those trades did exactly what it was designed to do: lose one unit of risk, −1.00R, stop hit as specified. The strategy never malfunctioned. The sizing had defined one unit of risk as half the account.

The answer to "can I use 20x" is therefore yes, sometimes, on the right trade. Fixed-fractional sizing at 1% risk does put on 24.8x gross — on trades where the stop is a fraction of a percent. It simply refuses to do it on RIVN. Leverage is what falls out of a risk budget divided by a stop distance. It is never a setting.

The one that survived

The proposition: a session closing outside what the options priced is information, not noise, and should therefore continue rather than revert. Testing it required fixing a mistake in the earlier study — signing forward returns by the trigger direction makes up moves look like they continue and down moves look like they continue down, when both are the same fact: everything drifts up.

It clears its null

The drift null captures 0.83 of 2.59 — 32% of the Sharpe, 25% of the mean R. For comparison, random entries captured 63–75% of the breakout book's result. This is the best-controlled positive finding of the session.

Both ways, one side tradeable

The short arm beats its own null (−0.09 against −0.79), and the down-move continuation is the larger effect in the event study. It still lands at zero. The information is there and is bigger on the down side; drift and cost consume all of it.

This is very likely post-earnings-announcement drift. A 3× implied move is nearly always an earnings surprise or a major announcement, and PEAD is among the most replicated anomalies there is. That makes the result more plausible, not less — but the honest next test is whether it survives outside earnings windows.

Busier option books do not trend harder

Two claims hide in the question, so both were measured: moving further (absolute forward move, volatility-scaled) and trending more cleanly (efficiency ratio — net change over the sum of the absolute daily changes).

Every measure is negative, and the sign is consistent — between 57% and 76% of names agree. When a name's option book runs hot against its own norm, what follows trends slightly worse and moves slightly less. That is consistent with the earlier finding that net GEX and gamma balance are negatively related to absolute return: dealers long gamma suppress movement.

The between-name number is a trap

Across the 42 names, volume-against-open-interest correlates +0.379 with trend quality, which looks like a discovery. The five most option-driven names are UVXY, SPY, QQQ, GLD and IWM — index products. The correlation is measuring instrument type, not option activity, and the underlying efficiency ratios barely differ at all: 0.257 at the top against 0.229 at the bottom.

42 US-listed names on split-adjusted five-minute bars, 2017–2026, resampled session-anchored; 143 names on split-adjusted daily bars, 1999–2026, for the model's wide cut. Option features are end-of-day chain snapshots read one session in arrears. Long only except where a short arm is named. Costs are per leg — 5bp taker, 2.5bp maker — charged by the order type that actually fills each leg. Every arm is matched to an 18% median drawdown before its return is read, and Sharpe bands are the 5th to 95th percentile over twenty capacity orderings, so an unpaired difference inside a band settles nothing. Where both stop and target are touched inside one bar the order is unknowable and the stop is charged. The 42-name universe was selected with hindsight and inflates mean R by roughly four times against the 143-name set; no result here has an out-of-sample split.
