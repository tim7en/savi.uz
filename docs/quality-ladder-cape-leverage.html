I - Two-account specification

Permanent capital and opportunity capital have different jobs.

Begin with $10,000 at 80% SPY core and 20% Treasury. Add $10,000 at the first session of every later year and an additional $30,000 in contribution years 3, 6, 9 and so on. Total contributed was $620,000 across 31 later deposits.

CAPE <25Drawdown-time SPY injection enters at 3x.
25-35Drawdown-time SPY injection enters at 2x.
CAPE >35New capital buys SPY at 1x.

NAV is the gate: the permanent 80% SPY core stays at 1x. If the prior-close flow-adjusted account NAV is less than 10% below its high, a new contribution also enters at 1x regardless of CAPE. At a drawdown of 10% or more, CAPE determines only the leverage of that new SPY tranche. The remaining 20% of every contribution enters Treasury.

Recovery is the reset: each leveraged injection holds its entry exposure until the combined account regains the high that preceded the drawdown. On the following session it is converted to 1x and merged into the permanent core. Monthly CAPE is lagged one month, and exposure above 1x pays prior-known DGS3MO +1%.

VIX is a dynamic ceiling: average the prior closes over 60 sessions, rank that smoothed value against its preceding 252 observations, and act no earlier than the following session. Below the 70th percentile, the CAPE ceiling is available; from the 70th through 90th percentile, cap the tranche at 2x; at or above the 90th, cap it at 1x. The raw, 5-session, 20-session and monthly-held versions are retained as controls.

The Treasury ladder remains unchanged: quality at -10%, SPY at -20%, quality at -30%, and SPY at -50% of SPY drawdown. Those rescue tranches remain unlevered.

This is a daily-rebalanced leverage model, not a promise that a leveraged ETF, futures account or margin account would reproduce it. ETF tracking, margin liquidation and taxes are omitted.

II - P&L versus SPY

The 60-session signal improved this historical path.

The 60-session VIX version produced $3,499,424 of cumulative net gain. It finished 2.49% versus the no-VIX dual guard and 1.49% versus SPY with identical deposits.

Risk result: maximum drawdown improved by 0.27 percentage point, from -52.60% to -52.33%. Annualized volatility declined from 17.70% to 17.60%.

The 20% Treasury sleeve is therefore insurance and dry powder, not a long-run return competitor. Its economic cost is the foregone equity compounding visible above.

VIII - Decision

Keep the 60-session VIX candidate, but do not crown it.

Smoothing fixed the operational chatter and the 60-session result held up in the post-2007 sensitivity. However, it was selected from four alternatives on the same history.

Bottom line: freeze 60 sessions before any further test, charge switching costs, and pair it with a 1.5x account-level exposure cap. If it survives rolling or genuinely out-of-sample evaluation, it can graduate from dashboard context to a rule.
