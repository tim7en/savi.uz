I · Exact rulebook

Deposits do not reset the drawdown clock.

Start with $10,000 and allocate 80% to the financed SPY sleeve and 20% to Treasury. Add $10,000 at the first close of every later calendar year and another $30,000 in contribution years 3, 6, 9, and so on. Each deposit is split 80/20.

−10%Deploy 10% of episode-start Treasury into unlevered SPY.
−20%Deploy another 20% into unlevered SPY.
−30%Deploy another 30% into unlevered SPY.
−40%Deploy remaining Treasury into 5–7 qualifying quality stocks.

Core leverage begins at 3x, falls to 2x at a 15% flow-adjusted NAV drawdown and to 1x at 30%. From 1x it returns to 2x only above −10%; 3x returns only after NAV fully recovers. Ten percent of positive financed SPY-sleeve P&L is swept to Treasury quarterly.

At −40%, candidates need five years of history, positive point-in-time earnings, five-year total-return CAGR above SPY, and a drawdown at least five percentage points deeper than SPY. After SPY regains its pre-crisis high, a profitable stock can harvest 1% of current shares at quarter boundaries when its price is above the prior harvest price.

Financing is prior-known 3-month Treasury yield plus 1%; reserve earns the prior-known Treasury yield; stock trades cost 5 bp. Adjusted-close returns reinvest dividends. Taxes, margin calls, ETF tracking error, market impact, and intraday gaps are omitted.

IV · The quality gate

The five-name rule never fired.

The portfolio crossed the −40% rung on 23 March 2001 and 17 March 2020. Exactly two names passed on each date, below the required minimum of five, so strict execution left the remaining Treasury parked.

Sensitivity only: relaxing the minimum to two raised ending wealth to $6,930,150 (24.42% versus SPY), but it also deepened maximum drawdown to -58.33%. The 2020 picks were NVDA and META. Because the universe is today's surviving compounders, this uplift is highly exposed to survivorship and selection bias.
