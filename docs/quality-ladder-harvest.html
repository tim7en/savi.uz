Unlevered test - 1993-01-29 to 2024-09-30

The ladder worked. The harvest did not add return.

Starting 80% SPY and 20% Treasury reduced risk and the quality selections modestly beat an SPY-only deployment ladder. But repeated 5% harvesting lowered terminal wealth, never refilled the reserve, and left the strategy behind SPY.

I - Exact tested rule

Four rungs consume one Treasury snapshot.

Begin with $10,000 and add $10,000 monthly. Every deposit is split 80% SPY / 20% Treasury. At the latest SPY adjusted-total-return high, freeze the available Treasury as the episode budget. Signals use the prior close and trades occur at the next close.

-10%20% of episode Treasury to the point-in-time top seven.
-20%30% of episode Treasury to unlevered SPY.
-30%30% of episode Treasury to the point-in-time top seven.
-50%Final 20% of episode Treasury to unlevered SPY.

Each quality lot is compared with SPY from its own entry. At quarter-end and at a new rung, every additional 20 percentage points of relative wealth outperformance sells 5% of the lot's original shares. Proceeds rebuild the pre-fall Treasury dollar target; only proceeds above that target buy SPY. When lagged CAPE is at least 35, the reserve target is raised to at least 20% of current NAV.

A stock at +12% while SPY is +10% has only 1.8% relative wealth excess and does not trigger. At SPY +10%, a stock needs +32% to reach the 20% relative hurdle.

The strategy ended -3.8% behind SPY. It reduced maximum drawdown by 2.7% and volatility by 1.3%, but its XIRR was -0.19 percentage points lower. This is a smoother path, not an outperforming SPY substitute.

III - What produced value?

Selection helped slightly; harvesting gave some back.

The quality strategy finished 0.44% above the otherwise identical SPY-only ladder and 14.6% above static contribution-split 80/20. But leaving the quality positions untouched finished at $25,659,871: repeated harvesting reduced terminal wealth by -0.74%.

CAPE was inert. The CAPE-aware and no-CAPE paths were identical. All $285,253 of quality-sale proceeds were still needed for the ordinary refill target; zero reached SPY and CAPE redirected an additional $0.

IV - Treasury refill

The reserve never returned to 20/80.

Ending Treasury was $297,676 (1.17% of NAV), against a nominal refill target of $803,449. The shortfall was $505,773. No harvested proceeds crossed the waterfall into SPY.

This reveals a rule conflict: “rebuild the old Treasury dollar amount” does not guarantee restoration to 20% of a portfolio that has compounded. If 80/20 is the intended standing policy, it requires an explicit rebalance or a 20%-of-current-NAV target in every valuation regime—not only when CAPE is high.

Quality stocks still represented 6.66% of ending NAV and reached 7.46% at maximum. Underperforming lots never trigger a relative-outperformance harvest, while 5%-of-original-share sales require twenty qualifying bands for a complete exit.

VIII - Decision

Keep the ladder; revise the refill and exit rules.

The strongest result is the staged deployment itself: it moved the path materially above static 80/20 while keeping drawdown below SPY. The point-in-time quality selection added only a small in-sample increment, and harvesting winners into an underfilled reserve reduced compounding.

Next defensible test: preserve the four rungs, compare full 20%-of-current-NAV reserve restoration against the nominal-dollar target, and add a predefined exit for quality lots that never outperform SPY. Those are corrections to incomplete state rules; they should be tested separately rather than folded into this result.
