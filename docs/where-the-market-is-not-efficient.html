Where the Market Is Not Efficient

Thirty-one things were tested against their own controls. Every one that tried to predict failed. Everything that survived was about execution, sizing or timeframe. That pattern is the finding — the market prices information efficiently and prices frictions badly, and the frictions are what a systematic book can actually harvest.

The pattern in one table

Sorting every result by what it was trying to do, rather than by what it was called, makes the shape obvious.

Everything predictive failed

Dealer gamma, CFTC positioning, macro regime, earnings deterioration, volume bursts, volume-profile location, theme strength, relative-strength ranking, MA crossovers, option-surface machine learning, pre-breakout volume, range compression, single-name drawdown spillover, drawdown depth on single stocks, the cheap half of quality names, the bubble run-up filter.

Sixteen attempts to know something the price did not already say. All failed a reversal test, a null, or a redundancy check. This is the efficient-market result, and it is the bulk of the evidence.

Everything structural survived

Choosing a slower bar. Sizing by volatility. Halving into an adverse regime. Stopping the pyramid at two units. Entering with a resting limit rather than a stop. Not trading the short side. Requiring a real discount before buying an index fund.

None of these forecast anything. They exploit that cost, capacity and volatility are mispriced by the participant, not by the market — a book that pays 10bp where it could pay 5, or trades a 30-minute bar where 4 hours works better, is losing to arithmetic rather than to competition.

The single largest effect is real and untradeable

Worth its own section because it is the cleanest illustration of where the boundary sits.

Where a breakout bar closes within its own range separates outcomes more sharply than anything else measured: bottom quintile −1.203R with an 86.8% stop-out rate, top quintile +1.140R, monotone across five buckets, right tail +14.2 points, p < 0.001.

And it cannot be used. Acting on it requires waiting for the bar to close, which surrenders the channel-edge fill and costs 0.73 Sharpe — more than the selection is worth. The information exists, and it arrives exactly one bar after the moment when acting on it would have been free.

That is what efficiency looks like from the inside. Not an absence of signal, but a signal priced so that capturing it costs slightly more than it pays.

Where the edge actually lives

Conditioning on volatility shows the whole strategy in one picture — and it is not the picture the programme assumed.

A low-volatility momentum system

At VIX under 13 the book returns 76.8% annualised against SPY's 46.9%, running 141% exposure. Above VIX 18 it loses money. This is not crisis alpha; it is a calm-tape system that survives crises by shrinking.

The shrinking is arithmetic, not skill. Size is equity × risk / N, so tripling volatility cuts position size by two-thirds automatically. In March 2020 the book held 2.1 positions at 29% exposure against its normal six and ~100%.

The protection has a bill attached

It lost only 5.6% while SPY fell 33.8% in COVID — then made 23.3% in the rebound against SPY's 51.5%. After Q4 2018 it made 5.8% against SPY's 25.1%. Having de-risked at the bottom, a 55-bar channel cannot re-arm until price has rebuilt it.

Over a full round trip 2018 was close to a wash. Convexity that costs the rebound is insurance, not alpha, and should be priced as such.

What would break every number here

Survivorship, quantified rather than estimated

The universe is Binance's trad-FI list as it stands today. Splitting it by each name's own outcome: the strategy scores Sharpe 1.33 on the third that rose most, 0.87 in the middle, and 0.48 on the worst third — whose median member still returned +54%, because nothing in this universe was delisted.

The edge scales almost perfectly with how much the underlying went up. On a money-weighted basis the book returns 18.1% IRR against SPY's 15.0% with $1,000 monthly contributions — but that +3.1pp is the same order as the survivorship bias, before tax on 9,046 trades taxed annually while an index defers indefinitely.

No out-of-sample test has ever been run, on this or on anything else in the programme.
